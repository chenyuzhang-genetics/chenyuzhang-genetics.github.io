About

I study the genetic mechanisms that shape plant traits, and the evolutionary principles behind them.

I am a Lecturer at the College of Forestry, Central South University of Forestry and Technology. I received my PhD in 2025, jointly trained by Huazhong Agricultural University and the Tea Research Institute of the Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences, with CSC-funded training in the Laboratory of Genetics at Wageningen University. My work combines genomics, quantitative genetics, plant physiology and elemental imaging, and focuses on the economic crops of the genus Camellia.

Research

The question that drives my work

How did the economic crops of Camellia — tea, oil-camellia and ornamental camellias — come into being, and what genetic mechanisms underlie their distinctive traits?

SNP chips · population genetics

Diversity of tea germplasm

Genotyping natural tea resources from across China to describe their diversity and link candidate genes to traits.

Genomics · flowering

Flowering in oil-camellia

Genome resources, SNP chips and QTL analysis to understand flowering time and inflorescence traits in Camellia oil crops.

QTL mapping · F1 population

Genetics of shoot quality traits

Mapping loci that control mineral elements, catechins and alkaloids in young tea shoots.

Ionomics · root physiology

Fluoride and aluminium in tea roots

How fluoride and aluminium interact in the root cell wall, and how oxalate and its transporters decide where fluoride ends up.
